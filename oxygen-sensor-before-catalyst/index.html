P0130, P2195 and related

Oxygen sensor before the catalytic converter (sensor 1)

Fault codes P0130, P2195 and related explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: MediumDrive gently and have it repaired soon. Leaving it can lead to bigger faults or higher fuel use.

What it means

The first oxygen sensor sits in the exhaust before the catalytic converter. It tells the computer whether the engine is burning too much or too little fuel, many times a second, and the computer trims the fuel to match. A slow, stuck or dead sensor leaves the engine guessing.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Higher fuel use
  • Rough idle or slight hesitation
  • Failed emission test
  • Often no symptom at all besides the warning light

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • An old sensor: they wear out with age and distance
  • An exhaust leak near the sensor
  • Contamination from coolant or oil being burnt
  • Wiring damaged by exhaust heat
  • A real mixture problem, such as a vacuum leak or leaking injector

What to check first

  • Listen for a ticking exhaust leak near the engine when cold
  • Check if lean or rich codes (P0171, P0172) are stored too: fix those first
  • A workshop can watch the sensor switching on a scan tool before replacing it

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
