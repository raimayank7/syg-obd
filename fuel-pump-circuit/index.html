P0230, P0627 and related

Fuel pump electrical circuit

Fault codes P0230, P0627 and related explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: HighThis can damage the engine or gearbox, or leave you stranded. Stop if the car feels unsafe, and have it checked now.

What it means

The electric fuel pump in or near the tank is switched by a relay or a control module. These codes are about that electrical supply, not about fuel pressure itself. If the pump loses power, the engine stops.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Engine cranks but will not start
  • Engine cuts out while driving
  • No humming sound from the tank when the ignition is switched on

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A failed fuel pump relay
  • A blown fuse
  • Corroded connector at the pump or tank
  • A failing pump drawing too much current
  • A tripped fuel cut-off (inertia) switch after a bump, on cars that have one

What to check first

  • Turn the ignition on and listen near the tank for two seconds of humming
  • Check the fuel pump fuse and relay named in the owner's manual
  • Because the car may stop without warning, repair it before any long trip

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
