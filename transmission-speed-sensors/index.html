P0715 to P0723

Transmission input and output speed sensors

Fault codes P0715 to P0723 explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: MediumDrive gently and have it repaired soon. Leaving it can lead to bigger faults or higher fuel use.

What it means

An automatic gearbox has a sensor on its input shaft and one on its output shaft. By comparing them the computer knows which gear is engaged and whether anything is slipping. If either signal is lost, it cannot time the gear changes.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Harsh or erratic gear changes
  • Gearbox stuck in one gear
  • Speedometer not working (output sensor on some cars)

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A failed speed sensor
  • Metal particles stuck to the sensor tip
  • Damaged wiring or a corroded connector
  • Low or dirty fluid

What to check first

  • These sensors are often simple to replace from outside the gearbox, but on some they are inside
  • Have the fluid level and condition checked at the same time

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
