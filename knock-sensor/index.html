P0325 to P0334

Knock sensor

Fault codes P0325 to P0334 explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: MediumDrive gently and have it repaired soon. Leaving it can lead to bigger faults or higher fuel use.

What it means

A knock sensor is a small microphone bolted to the engine. It listens for knock, a harmful rattling kind of combustion, so the computer can back off the ignition timing. Without a good signal the computer plays safe and retards the timing all the time.

CodeMeaning

P0332Knock sensor 2 signal low, bank 2
P0333Knock sensor 2 signal high, bank 2
P0334Knock sensor 2 signal cuts in and out, bank 2

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Slightly less power
  • Slightly higher fuel use
  • Pinging sound under load in some cases

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A failed sensor
  • Corroded connector or damaged wiring, often heat or rodent damage
  • A sensor that is loose or over-tightened
  • Real engine knock from poor fuel or carbon build-up

What to check first

  • Use fuel of the octane grade the maker recommends
  • The sensor is often hard to reach, so replacement is usually a workshop job

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
