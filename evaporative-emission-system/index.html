P0440 to P0457

Fuel vapour (EVAP) system

Fault codes P0440 to P0457 explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: LowUsually safe to keep driving. Repair it when convenient, and do not ignore it for months.

What it means

Petrol gives off vapour. The EVAP system traps it in a charcoal canister and later feeds it to the engine to be burnt, so it does not escape into the air. The computer tests the system for leaks. The most common cause of a leak code is simply a fuel cap that is loose or has a worn seal.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Usually only the warning light
  • Smell of petrol near the car
  • Hard starting just after refuelling (purge valve stuck open)

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A loose, damaged or wrong fuel cap
  • A cracked hose or filler neck
  • A purge valve stuck open or closed
  • A faulty vent valve, often blocked with dust
  • A leaking charcoal canister

What to check first

  • Remove the fuel cap and refit it until it clicks
  • Check the rubber seal on the cap for cracks
  • The light may take several days of driving to go out after the cap is fixed
  • Do not keep filling after the pump clicks off: overfilling floods the canister

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.

Related codes

Exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) (P0400), Secondary air injection (P0410), Catalytic converter efficiency (P0420), Diesel particulate filter (DPF) (P2002), Diesel exhaust fluid (AdBlue) and SCR system (P203F). All codes.

Written in November 2026. These are generic OBD-II codes, the same on all makes. Causes and repair steps differ between models.
